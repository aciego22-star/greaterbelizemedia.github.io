Lobster season runs to the end of February and conch to the end of June, though
either can close early once a quota is reached, so it is worth confirming the
dates before you plan around them. Between now and then the reef is busy, and
most of the gear that lets you down out there is gear that was left wet in a
bag since the last trip.

Here is what we stock for the water, and what to look at before you go in.

## Start with the gun, and the shaft behind it

Spearfishing in Belize is done on a breath, not on a tank, so the gun matters
less for raw power than for how it handles when you are working a reef edge on
one lungful of air.

Our [speargun](/products/speargun) is band powered and sized for free-diving
the reef rather than blue water. Shorter guns track faster in close, which is
most of the shooting people actually do here. Keep a spare shaft with you:
[barbed speargun poles](/products/speargun-poles) are the part most often bent
or lost on a rock, and a trip ends quickly without one.

If you shoot a pole spear or a sling instead, we carry
[surgical tubing](/products/surgical-tubing) for rigging and repairs.

## Bands and tubing are what fail first

This is the part people underestimate. Latex does not care how carefully you
store a gun. Sun, salt and Belize heat work on rubber constantly, and a band
that looks fine can let go on the shot.

Check yours for cracking at the wishbone, for a chalky surface, and for
softness that was not there last season. If you find any of the three, replace
them. We keep [speargun bands](/products/speargun-bands) and
[speargun barbs](/products/speargun-barbs) in stock precisely because these are
consumables rather than fittings, and it is worth holding a spare set at home
rather than discovering it on the boat.

## Mask, fins and booties decide the dive

On a breath hold, comfort is not a luxury. A mask that leaks costs you air
every time you clear it, and fins that rub will end a day early.

A [mask and snorkel set](/products/mask-snorkel-set) with tempered glass is the
standard choice for reef swimming and free-diving. If you are snorkelling on
the surface rather than diving down, the
[full-face snorkel mask](/products/full-face-snorkel-mask) gives a wider, clearer
view and suits anyone who finds a mouthpiece tiring.

Our [dive fins](/products/dive-fins) suit free-diving, spearfishing and general
reef swimming. Pair open-heel fins with
[dive booties](/products/dive-booties), which also save your feet on a rocky
shore entry, something anyone who has walked in off the wrong beach will
recognise.

Come and try the fit in the shop. Fins and masks are not worth ordering blind.

## Rinse it, dry it, then put it away

Everything above lasts longer with ten minutes of attention at the end of the
day. Rinse the gun, the shaft, the bands, the mask and the fins in fresh water,
then let them dry out of direct sun before anything goes back in a bag.

Store bands slack rather than under tension, and keep rubber out of the
sunlight between trips. We went through this in more detail for boats and
tackle in our guide on
[caring for your gear in Belize’s saltwater](/blog/belize-saltwater-care), and
the same principles apply underwater.

## Know where you can use it

Spearfishing here is licensed, and spearguns are not permitted everywhere.
Marine reserves and certain zones have their own restrictions, and taking fish
on SCUBA is a separate matter again.

The Belize Fisheries Department sets and publishes these rules, and they are
the ones to confirm with before a trip, particularly if you are heading
somewhere new or you are visiting. Rules change, and we would rather you asked
than guessed. If you are unsure, ask us in the shop as well and we will point
you in the right direction.
