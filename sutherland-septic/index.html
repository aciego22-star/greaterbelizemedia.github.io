Septic Care Learning Center

Know Your System, Protect Your Property

Straightforward answers from our team to help you avoid backups, surprises and expensive repairs.

How do I know my tank needs pumping?

Slow drains, gurgling pipes, odors near the tank or a soggy drain field are all early warning signs.

Learn More
If you notice several drains running slowly at once, wastewater pooling in the yard, or unpleasant smells indoors or outside, your tank is likely full. A quick inspection confirms it before a backup happens.

How often should I pump my septic system?

Most households benefit from pumping every 2–3 years, depending on tank size and usage.

Learn More
Larger families and busy commercial properties fill their tanks faster and may need service more often. We'll recommend a schedule that fits how your property is actually used.

Why is my tank filling quickly?

Excess water use, leaks, or solids building up faster than expected can all fill a tank early.

Learn More
Running toilets, dripping fixtures and heavy laundry days send extra water to the tank. Flushing non-degradable items also fills it faster. We can help pinpoint the cause during service.

Can grease damage my septic system?

Yes. Grease and oils harden and clog pipes, drain fields and the tank itself over time.

Learn More
Never pour cooking grease down the drain. It congeals inside the system and blocks flow, leading to backups and expensive repairs. Restaurants especially benefit from a scheduled maintenance plan.

Is my drain field failing?

Standing water, bright green patches or lingering odors over the field may signal trouble.

Learn More
A struggling drain field is easier to save when caught early. If you notice soggy ground or slow drains that return after pumping, book an inspection right away.

How do I prevent expensive repairs?

Regular pumping, smart water use and routine inspections are the best protection you have.

Learn More
A little prevention goes a long way. Staying on a pumping schedule and catching small issues early costs far less than emergency repairs or a full system replacement.
