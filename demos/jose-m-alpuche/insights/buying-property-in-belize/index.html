Buying property in Belize

A listing can help you decide whether a property is worth visiting. Before you decide whether it is worth buying, make sure the property you have seen is the same one described in the title documents, the agreement and any survey. Those documents answer different questions about the same transaction.

Start with the property and the seller

Ask for the parcel or title reference and the seller’s title documents. Tell your attorney if the person negotiating is someone other than the recorded owner, or if the seller is acting through a company or representative. The first review can identify what needs to be checked about ownership, authority and any recorded interests. The Land Registry and Land Titles Unit describes its registry functions online.

If the property’s practical use depends on a road, a boundary, water access or development approval, mention that before the agreement is settled. A legal title review may need to be supplemented by advice from a surveyor, inspector, planner or other professional.

Know what the deposit commits you to

Find out who will hold the deposit, when it is payable and when it is refundable. If your plans depend on financing, satisfactory title checks or another event, ask counsel how the contract addresses that condition. Review what happens if either party needs more time or a condition cannot be met. These questions are easier to address while the terms are still open.

Work out the closing sequence

Ask what has to happen before the balance is paid and who is responsible for each step. Allow for legal costs, any applicable duties or charges, registry fees and other professional costs. Ask what you will receive at closing and what will confirm that the transfer has been recorded.

For transactions between a resident and a non-resident, the Central Bank of Belize publishes prior-notice guidance. If you will sign abroad or send funds from another country, discuss the arrangements before setting the closing date.

Have these points handy when you call

  • A parcel reference or property description.
  • The proposed buyer and seller and whether either is acting through a company.
  • The proposed price, deposit, conditions and closing date.
  • Anything you have signed or agreed to already.
  • Any question about access, boundaries, financing or overseas signing.

For general information only. The records, checks and transfer steps depend on the property and transaction.
