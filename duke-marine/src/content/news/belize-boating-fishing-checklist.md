Belize offers exceptional opportunities for fishing, boating and diving.
However, even the best day on the water can be interrupted by a weak battery,
damaged line, missing safety equipment or one important item left at home.

In the first instalment of our On the Water Guide, we explored
[Why Belize Gives You Your Best Fishing Experience](/blog/why-belize-best-fishing).
The next step is making sure your boat and equipment are ready before you leave
the dock.

## Begin with the boat

A boat that starts at the dock is not necessarily prepared for a full day on the
water. Before departure, check your fuel, battery, electrical switches,
navigation lights, bilge pump and essential plumbing.

Inspect your anchor, chain, rope and swivels for wear or corrosion. Dock lines
and fenders should also be accessible and in good condition, especially when
leaving or returning in changing conditions.

If you are towing the boat, inspect the trailer lights, rollers, tyres, coupling
and securing points before beginning the journey.

## Match your fishing gear to the trip

Your equipment should reflect the species and water you plan to fish. A flats
setup will differ from equipment intended for reef fishing, trolling or deeper
offshore water.

Check that your rods and reels are operating smoothly. Inspect your line for
abrasion, test the drag and replace any leader that has become cloudy, curled or
damaged. Confirm that you have the appropriate hooks, swivels, sinkers, lures and
terminal tackle before leaving shore.

It is also helpful to carry a small selection of alternatives. Conditions can
change, and fish may respond differently throughout the day.

## Check your essential safety equipment

Every person aboard should have suitable flotation equipment. Your communication
devices should be charged, protected from water and ready for use.

A practical safety check should also include your first-aid supplies, lighting,
drinking water, sun protection and an emergency means of attracting attention.
Check the expected weather and sea conditions, and ensure someone ashore knows
your intended route and approximate return time.

## Prepare for saltwater exposure

Saltwater, heat and sunlight place constant pressure on marine equipment. Small
signs of corrosion, loose hardware or worn rope should not be ignored.

After each trip, rinse rods, reels, metal fittings and other exposed equipment
with fresh water. Allow everything to dry properly before storage. Regular
inspection and simple maintenance can help identify developing problems before
they interfere with a future trip.

## Ask before you head out

Preparation does not need to be complicated. It begins with knowing where you are
going, what you are targeting and what equipment the trip requires.
