## Getting ready for your first dental visit

**Route** /blog/getting-ready-for-your-first-dental-visit

**Excerpt** A short list of questions and useful details can make a first visit easier to prepare for.

### Choose what you’d like to cover

Start with a simple question. Are you hoping to schedule a cleaning, get a tooth checked, or understand a treatment you’ve heard about? If you have several things in mind, put the most important one first. A consultation is an opportunity to describe what matters to you and get help working out the order in which to address it.

### Ask what to bring

Call the clinic before your visit if you have recent X-rays or records from another dentist. Ask what the team needs and how they prefer to receive it. If there is a retainer or denture you want the dentist to assess, ask whether to bring it. Having that conversation ahead of time can save you from guessing what will be useful on the day.

### Tell the team what would make you feel more comfortable

Some patients like to hear what is going to happen before each step. Others prefer to ask their questions first and talk through what the dentist finds at the end. If you’re anxious or concerned about sensitivity, say so when you arrive. Ask how you can signal that you would like to pause or take a moment during the visit.

### Leave with a clear next step

Before you go, ask what the dentist found, whether there is a decision to make, and whether you need another visit. If a treatment is proposed, ask what the appointment would involve and when you can get information about its cost. You can also ask who to call if a question comes up after you leave.

## What should I ask about retainers?

**Route** /blog/what-to-ask-about-retainers

**Excerpt** Questions to bring when you’re discussing how a retainer fits into life after orthodontic treatment.

### Start with the kind of retainer you have

Retainers are used to help keep teeth in position after orthodontic treatment. A fixed retainer is bonded to the inside of the teeth. A removable retainer can be taken out for cleaning and worn on the schedule recommended by an orthodontist. The kind you have affects what to ask at a review.

### Talk through the practical details

For a fixed retainer, ask your orthodontist to show you how they recommend cleaning around it. For a removable one, ask how it should feel when it fits well, how to care for it, and what wear schedule applies to you. You can also ask when to bring the retainer back for a check.

### Bring the appliance if something has changed

If a removable retainer feels different or a fixed one seems to have loosened, contact the practice and explain what changed. Bring the retainer or any pieces you still have if the team asks you to. An assessment lets the orthodontist check the appliance and discuss whether any work, replacement, or follow-up is appropriate.

### Find out how to reach the team

When a retainer is fitted or checked, ask the office how to schedule a review if it changes fit or gets lost. If your original treatment was done elsewhere, ask what records would help the team understand your care. These are useful questions even if you don’t need a new appliance today.

## Preparing for a denture consultation

**Route** /blog/preparing-for-a-denture-consultation

**Excerpt** A guide to discussing a first denture, an interim replacement, or a denture you already wear.

### Describe what you want to improve

Identify the everyday situations you would like to make easier. That might include speaking comfortably, feeling more at ease while eating with others, or getting a better fit from a denture you already have. Specific examples give the dentist a place to start and make it easier to discuss what a new or adjusted appliance could help with.

### Ask which type of denture you’re discussing

Partial dentures replace some teeth in an arch. Full dentures replace all teeth in an upper or lower arch. An immediate denture can form part of a plan that includes tooth removal. Ask what the dentist has in mind for your situation and what would need to happen before a denture could be made or fitted.

### Make room for fitting and follow-up questions

Ask how the practice checks the fit, when follow-up might be useful, and who you can contact if you have questions while getting used to it. If the plan includes an immediate denture, ask how healing could affect its fit and whether adjustments may be needed.

### Bring what helps you explain the issue

If you already have a denture, ask whether the dentist would like you to bring it. Think of a few examples of where you notice the fit most, such as while talking or eating. Use the visit to get a picture of the decisions ahead and which ones can wait until after an examination.
